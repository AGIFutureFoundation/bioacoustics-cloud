The stack, in the open

Two open components anchor the DAO's engineering — a production conservation detector and the open core pipeline.

Conservation

🐳 Beluga Sounds

Machine learning that detects beluga whale calls in hydrophone recordings — built for the Cook Inlet population, the smallest of five U.S. beluga populations, down ~75% since 1979 and listed endangered in 2008 (~279 whales as of January 2020). Trained on 13,000+ hours of audio from Cook Inlet critical habitat, with a CNN ensemble — AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet50, and DenseNet — replacing a sensitive-but-noisy detector so conservation teams stop reviewing false positives. Collaborative work with Microsoft's AI for Earth and AI for Good Research Lab and the NOAA Alaska Fisheries Science Center.

Open core

🌊 Wild Animal Voice Engine

WAVE — developed on the Virtual Protocol to help train systems to become translators of all species. An open, MIT-licensed pipeline: ingest field audio from bio-logging sensors, resolve it through the Superlet Transform (sharp time and frequency at once — it revealed the Chagos pygmy blue whale song is pulsed, not continuous), and understand it with foundation models — NatureLM and Perch 2.0 — behind one swappable interface. Small, readable, and extendable end to end.

The Technical Triad

Three layers turn raw sound into structured, comparable meaning.

01 · Capture

Bio-logging & edge AI

Hydrophones, loggers, and sensors capture signal from dense environmental noise, with inference at the edge. On-device (edge) inference with sub-2MB TinyML models works offline at remote stations and on marine hydrophones — real-time species presence across land and sea.

02 · Resolve

The Superlet Transform

High-resolution signal processing adapted from neuroscience. It revealed the Chagos pygmy blue whale song is pulsed, not continuous. Higher time-frequency resolution than standard wavelet analysis, revealing fine structure inside calls — pulses, harmonics, transitions.

03 · Understand

Foundation models

NatureLM-audio and Perch 2.0 map vocal patterns without supervision — models trained on one species can help organize another's vocalizations. Zero-shot species detection across thousands of species, validated against the BEANS benchmark.
